# Design inspiration: US distribution services websites

Research date: 2026-10-08

This collection focuses on US-based distribution, warehousing, fulfillment, cold-chain, and logistics service businesses. The goal is to identify patterns that can help shape Poblaliment’s website, especially around trust, product discovery, supplier relationships, and making a physical operation feel easy to understand online.

The strongest examples do four things well:

- Explain the service in plain language before introducing operational detail.
- Turn scale, locations, technology, and service levels into visible proof.
- Give different audiences a clear path: customers, suppliers, carriers, and job seekers.
- Use real people, facilities, products, and customer stories instead of relying only on generic logistics imagery.

## Detailed references

### 1. Uber Freight — network intelligence with a clear commercial story

**Website:** [uberfreight.com/en-US](https://www.uberfreight.com/en-US)

**What to study:**

- The hero immediately states the category, the scale of the network, and the business outcome.
- Large numbers are used as a visual proof layer: freight under management, shipments, transactions, savings, and coverage.
- The main offer is reduced to three understandable solution groups: managed transportation, capacity solutions, and TMS.
- Industry navigation lets buyers self-select without forcing every visitor through the same generic sales funnel.
- Case studies appear as proof of outcomes rather than as an isolated resource archive.

**Useful pattern for Poblaliment:** Put the core promise first, then support it with a small number of verified facts: product categories, service area, years of activity, supplier count, delivery rhythm, or other real figures once they are confirmed.

**Do not copy directly:** The scale and enterprise language would make a local distributor feel distant. Keep the structure, but use warmer language and real local context.

### 2. Flexport — make a complex offer navigable

**Website:** [flexport.com](https://www.flexport.com/)

**What to study:**

- The information architecture separates the platform, operational services, use cases, business types, industries, tools, and resources.
- The website treats visibility and control as product benefits, not only as internal capabilities.
- B2B fulfillment is presented alongside freight forwarding, customs, inventory, parcel, returns, and replenishment, which helps show the full customer journey.
- Developer and product surfaces are visible for users who need a technical integration, while the main marketing story remains commercial.

**Useful pattern for Poblaliment:** Organize content around the questions a visitor has: what products are available, who supplies them, how the relationship works, where the service operates, and how to make contact.

**Do not copy directly:** A global platform needs a very large navigation system. Poblaliment should use the same grouping principle with four or five simple top-level routes.

### 3. GXO — industrial operations presented as innovation

**Website:** [gxo.com](https://gxo.com/)

**What to study:**

- The opening message pairs operational credibility with innovation, robotics, and tailored solutions.
- Technology is treated as a benefit for customers rather than as a list of equipment.
- The page moves from solutions to industries to people and then to proof, creating a logical narrative from capability to trust.
- Scale metrics and operational results give the page a strong visual rhythm.
- Video and facility imagery help turn warehouse work into something tangible.

**Useful pattern for Poblaliment:** Show the work behind the service: receiving products, selecting orders, preparing deliveries, and caring for the supplier relationship. A few authentic process images can do more than a generic warehouse hero image.

**Do not copy directly:** GXO’s scale depends on corporate proof and complex operations. Poblaliment can achieve the same confidence through specificity and human presence.

### 4. Lineage — own a specialist category

**Website:** [onelineage.com](https://www.onelineage.com/)

**What to study:**

- The service menu is deep but organized into two useful families: global warehousing and integrated solutions.
- “Find a facility” is a prominent action because location is central to the buying decision.
- Technology, sustainability, case studies, and educational content are treated as part of the service experience.
- The company explains a specialist category through clear sub-services such as cold storage, port-centric warehousing, automated warehousing, and food solutions.

**Useful pattern for Poblaliment:** Make supplier and product discovery feel like a first-class experience. If visitors need to understand who is behind the products, create a dedicated supplier or brands area with short, consistent profiles.

**Do not copy directly:** Lineage has a global network and a specialist cold-chain story. Poblaliment should make its own local and product-led point of view the differentiator.

### 5. NFI Industries — combine heritage with a human service promise

**Website:** [nfiindustries.com](https://www.nfiindustries.com/)

**What to study:**

- The headline “One Partner. Every Move. Infinite Possibilities.” compresses a broad offer into a memorable idea.
- “A Culture of Service” gives the operation a human frame before the website moves into capabilities.
- The solution grid makes a wide service portfolio scannable: distribution, ecommerce, transportation, port services, brokerage, intermodal, and real estate.
- Historical imagery and a timeline make longevity visible without turning the whole site into a corporate history page.
- News and insight content keeps the company current after the first visit.

**Useful pattern for Poblaliment:** Use a short positioning line, a service or supplier overview, and a small “why work with us” section that explains the company’s way of working.

**Do not copy directly:** The number of service lines is much larger than Poblaliment’s initial scope. Avoid a grid that makes the company look more complex than it is.

### 6. ITS Logistics — a strong model for a distribution page

**Website:** [its4logistics.com/services/distribution-fulfillment](https://www.its4logistics.com/services/distribution-fulfillment/)

**What to study:**

- The hero says exactly what the service is and names the delivery contexts it supports: B2B, D2C, retail, and omnichannel.
- The page explains custom-built solutions and technology before presenting the network footprint.
- Facility cards make the network concrete with locations, square footage, access, and regional advantages.
- Distribution capabilities are broken into recognizable terms such as retail distribution, omnichannel distribution, supply chain integration, vendor compliance, and routing guides.
- Case studies and news sit below the service story, where they support credibility without blocking the primary conversion path.

**Useful pattern for Poblaliment:** This is the closest reference for a service page structure: promise → how it works → locations or product coverage → capabilities → proof → contact.

**Do not copy directly:** Avoid publishing operational metrics until they are verified. Use real facts from the business and remove sections that cannot be supported.

### 7. ShipBob — translate fulfillment into business growth

**Website:** [shipbob.com](https://www.shipbob.com/)

**What to study:**

- Fulfillment is framed around the customer’s growth, not warehouse terminology alone.
- The website is designed to help a visitor understand the service quickly, then move toward a quote or conversation.
- Product, technology, integrations, and operational services are connected into one customer journey.
- Ecommerce language is specific enough to help a brand recognize itself in the offer.

**Useful pattern for Poblaliment:** Talk about the result that matters to the audience. For suppliers and collaborators, that may be reach, consistency, visibility, local relationships, or reliable product presentation.

**Do not copy directly:** ShipBob is optimized for ecommerce brands and a digital self-serve funnel. Poblaliment can keep the clarity while making contact more personal.

### 8. Saltbox — make warehouse infrastructure feel human

**Website:** [saltbox.com](https://www.saltbox.com/)

**What to study:**

- The company positions physical space and operational support as an approachable experience for product-based businesses.
- The visual language is more editorial and lifestyle-oriented than traditional freight sites.
- Community, flexibility, and people help balance the functional warehouse offer.
- The brand shows that an operations business can feel modern without depending on futuristic technology graphics.

**Useful pattern for Poblaliment:** Use a warmer visual system: real product details, hands, shelves, deliveries, and people who represent the relationship behind the service.

**Do not copy directly:** Saltbox serves a different audience and business model. Borrow its tone and photography discipline rather than its exact messaging.

### 9. Kenco — explain operational detail without losing the main story

**Website:** [kencogroup.com/solutions/warehousing-distribution](https://kencogroup.com/solutions/warehousing-distribution/)

**What to study:**

- The page begins with the business outcomes: efficiency, accuracy, scalability, inventory control, fulfillment speed, and service performance.
- Dedicated and shared warehousing are explained in plain language.
- Network design, distribution technology, safety, and integrated services are distinct sections rather than one long list.
- The site includes a customer testimonial and measurable safety proof to make operational claims more credible.

**Useful pattern for Poblaliment:** When a service needs detail, give each capability a one-sentence explanation and one piece of evidence. This keeps the page useful to a serious buyer without turning it into a technical manual.

**Do not copy directly:** Kenco includes a large operational footprint and many service types. Keep the hierarchy, but reduce the number of sections for a small public-facing site.

### 10. Americold — turn a network into a story

**Website:** [americold.com](https://www.americold.com/)

**What to study:**

- The opening idea, “Cold Chain Solutions that Make More Possible,” gives a specialist service a human and commercial meaning.
- The network is visualized by geography, facilities, pallet capacity, and services.
- Expertise is grouped into recognizable pillars: cold supply chain, technology and automation, value-added services, import/export, custom solutions, and sustainability.
- Operational proof is presented through scale, history, product movement, storage capacity, and customer relationships.
- Food imagery and people keep the subject connected to the end result, not only to infrastructure.

**Useful pattern for Poblaliment:** Connect distribution to the products and people it serves. If Poblaliment represents food suppliers, the site should show how products move from producer to customer and why that relationship matters.

## Patterns worth carrying into Poblaliment

### 1. Use a simple promise before the service list

The first screen should answer three questions quickly:

1. What does Poblaliment do?
2. Who does it help?
3. What should the visitor do next?

A useful structure is:

> A short statement about connecting food products and people in the local market.
>
> One supporting sentence that explains the supplier, product, or distribution relationship.
>
> A single primary action such as “Discover our suppliers” or “Get in touch”.

The final wording should use verified business language once the positioning is confirmed.

### 2. Make supplier discovery the main experience

For a food distribution business, supplier and brand discovery can carry more identity than a generic “services” page. Consider a consistent supplier card with:

- Supplier or brand name.
- Product category.
- Short description of the relationship or offer.
- One real product or supplier image.
- Link to a detail page when more information is available.

Keep the first version selective. A smaller set of well-presented suppliers will feel more intentional than a dense catalogue with incomplete information.

### 3. Replace generic proof with local proof

Use facts that can be checked and maintained:

- Area served.
- Product categories.
- Supplier or producer relationships.
- Years operating.
- Delivery or service model.
- Contact response expectation.

Do not add warehouse square footage, customer counts, delivery percentages, or market coverage until the business confirms them.

### 4. Show the real operation

The strongest visual direction for Poblaliment is likely a mix of:

- Close product photography.
- Supplier or producer portraits.
- Hands, crates, shelves, labels, and delivery details.
- A small number of wide contextual images.
- Simple maps or route graphics only when they explain a real service area.

The repository already contains food and product photographs. Their subjects, licensing, and editorial use should be confirmed before they become public-facing content.

### 5. Keep the navigation small

A first release can work with:

- Inici
- Nosaltres
- Les nostres marques or Proveïdors
- Contacte

The website can borrow the grouping discipline of Flexport or Lineage without inheriting their enterprise-scale navigation. The primary action should remain visible in the header and repeated after the main supplier or product story.

### 6. Use a warm, confident visual system

The reference sites suggest a useful balance for Poblaliment:

- Use bold typography and generous spacing for confidence.
- Use a restrained base palette so the food photography provides colour.
- Add one distinctive accent colour drawn from the brand or local context.
- Prefer real photography and clear graphic shapes over generic truck or container imagery.
- Use motion to reveal content and guide attention, with reduced-motion support.

The final typeface, colours, logo treatment, and image direction should wait for confirmed brand material.

## Suggested homepage sequence

1. **Opening promise:** what Poblaliment connects and for whom.
2. **Supplier or product introduction:** make the offer visible early.
3. **How the relationship works:** a simple three-step explanation.
4. **Selected suppliers or product categories:** real cards with concise copy.
5. **Why Poblaliment:** local knowledge, trust, care, reach, or other confirmed differentiators.
6. **A human or product story:** one image-led section that gives the business character.
7. **Contact call to action:** an easy next step with the information people need to respond.

## What to borrow and what to avoid

| Borrow | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| Clear outcome-led headlines | “End-to-end solutions” without an explanation |
| Facility, network, or supplier proof | Unverified scale numbers |
| Real people and products | Generic stock photos of trucks and containers |
| Audience paths and focused calls to action | Sending every visitor to the same vague contact form |
| Modular service or supplier cards | Dense grids with equal visual weight everywhere |
| Case studies and stories | Claims that cannot be traced to a real customer or supplier |
| A visual map when geography matters | Decorative maps that do not explain coverage |

## Recommended starting direction

Use **ITS Logistics** as the structural reference for a clear distribution story, **NFI** for the combination of people and heritage, **Americold** for food and network storytelling, and **Saltbox** for a more contemporary and approachable tone.

For Poblaliment, the resulting site should feel local, editorial, and dependable: a clear introduction to the company, an inviting way to discover suppliers and products, and a direct route to a real conversation.
